## Authority, access and bounded inspection

William Lilly, *Christian Astrology*, London, 1647. S01 is the Wellcome
photographed scan `b30338724` (Public Domain Mark); S02 is the
[EEBO-derived scan](https://archive.org/details/bim_early-english-books-1641-1700_christian-astrology-_lilly-william_1647)
of the same edition. OCR was not used for any reading below; every reading
comes from the photographs, including planetary and sign glyphs.

This pass read the perfection passages on printed pp.110, 111, 112, 113, 124,
125, 126 and 127 in both witnesses: 16 images, 8 printed pages. It was one
model-assisted reading/collation pass, not independent human review or
double-blind transcription. The two witnesses are photographs of one edition,
not independent doctrinal authorities.

S01 bytes were copied only after matching the hashes recorded in the original
packet (`packet.json`). S02 pp.110, 112, 113 and 124 were reused from the P3
cache after hash verification. S02 pp.111, 125, 126 and 127 were acquired from
the BookReader image service; their candidate leaves (P3's checked +29 offset)
were confirmed by reading the printed page number on each photograph. The
originals, acquisition script and receipt (`acquisition.json`, with exact URL,
locator, byte count and SHA-256 per image) remain under
`C:/dev/outputs/lilly-p4-perfection-20261030/`.

## Passage findings

Compact paraphrases of the photographed text. "Both" means the reading is the
same in S01 and S02.

| ID | Page | Reading (both unless noted) | Consequence |
|---|---|---|---|
| S4-01 | 110 | After separation, the number of degrees the swifter planet still needs to be wholly separated from the more ponderous gives weeks, days, months or years until the parties desist. Significators in movable signs, angular and swift hasten; common signs take longer; fixed longer still. | Timing qualifiers concern separation and dissolution. They give units without a selection rule; no degree-to-time conversion is admitted (A23). |
| S4-02 | 110-111 | Prohibition: two planets signifying the matter are applying to an aspect, and before they reach a true aspect another planet interposes his body or aspect, so the matter is hindered and retarded. | The definition names the interposition, not which significator it touches. Its stated effect is hindrance and retardation. |
| S4-03 | 111 | Bodily example: Mars 7 Aries applies to Saturn 12 Aries; the Sun at 6 Aries, swifter than Mars, overtakes Mars and conjoins Saturn first, "first impediting" Mars and then Saturn. Combustion is called the greatest misfortune. | The example's interposer contacts both significators in order, beginning with the applying one. Speeds are asserted, not given. |
| S4-04 | 111 | Aspect example: two planets going to conjunction; Mars 7 Aries, Saturn in Aries, Sun 5 Gemini. The Sun, swifter in diurnal motion, passes the dexter sextile of Mars and reaches a dexter sextile of Saturn before Mars reaches Saturn. The same applies to square, trine and opposition. | **Witness variant V1:** Saturn is "15" of Aries in S01 and "13" in S02. Both readings give a coherent ordering; neither is emended. |
| S4-05 | 111 | Refranation (prohibition "more properly called" refranation): Saturn 12 Aries, Mars 7; Mars hastens to the conjunction but turns retrograde before the tenth or eleventh degree and refrains; Saturn still moves forward; nothing signified by the former conjunction will ever be effected. | **Witness variant V2:** the sign glyph after "eleventh degree of" is Aries in S01 and a damaged or different glyph in S02. Context requires Aries; S02's glyph is recorded, not read. The stated effect is permanent non-effecting. |
| S4-06 | 111 | Translation: a light planet separates from a more weighty one and presently joins another more heavy. Example: Saturn 20 Aries, Mars 15, Mercury 16; Mercury, swift, separates from Mars and translates Mars's virtue to Saturn. Done by any aspect as well as by conjunction. Meaning: a person signified by the translator procures assistance. | No reception or principal-history condition here. The translator is lighter than both partners. Mars is itself applying to Saturn in the example. |
| S4-07 | 112 | Reception: significators in each other's dignity; by house strongest; may be by triplicity, term or face, or any essential dignity. Triplicity example holds "if the Question or Nativity be by day"; the term example's second sign glyph is blotted in both photographs. | Mutual reception is the defined case; direction and basis are explicit. Sect enters triplicity reception. |
| S4-08 | 112 | When aspects deny the matter, when the significators have no aspect, or when a square or opposition makes it doubtful, mutual reception between the principal significators brings it to pass without great trouble and suddenly, to both parties' content. | A reception route without (or despite) aspects. "Denied by the aspects" does not say whether prohibition, refranation or frustration are overcome (A09). |
| S4-09 | 112-113 | Frustration: a swift planet would corporally join a more ponderous one, but before they can conjoin the more weighty planet joins another. Example: Mercury 10 Aries, Mars 12, Jupiter 13; Mars reaches Jupiter first; Mercury is frustrated. Proverb: two dogs quarrel, a third gets the bone. | Bodily (conjunction) by definition. The intercepting contact is the heavier significator's own conjunction with a third, not an interposer's approach. |
| S4-10 | 113 | Saturn, Jupiter and Mars, above the Sun's orb, are the superior, ponderous and more weighty planets; Venus, Mercury and the Moon are inferior. | A class division. It does not rank within a class or place the Sun (A07). |
| S4-11 | 124 | Chap. XXI: whether a thing demanded will be perfected; the Ancients give four ways. After considering applications, separations, the Moon and the aspects, judge whether, by whose means, when, and whether it is good for the querent. | Perfection, agency, timing and desirability are separate questions. |
| S4-12 | 125 | Conjunction: Lord of the Ascendant and lord of the quesited house hastening to conjunction, in the first house or any angle, meeting no prohibition or refranation before the perfect conjunction: brought to pass without let; sooner if swift and essentially or accidentally strong. In a succedent house: perfected, not so soon. Cadent: infinite loss of time, some difficulty, much struggling. | House class qualifies ease and speed; it does not deny the conjunction. The house of "this conjunction" and its epoch are unstated (A16). |
| S4-13 | 125 | Sextile or trine: applying out of good houses and places where they are essentially well dignified, with no malevolent aspect intervening before the partile sextile or trine. | "Good houses", "well dignified" and "malevolent aspect" are not defined here. |
| S4-14 | 125 | Square: perfection provided each planet has dignity in the degrees wherein it is and applies out of proper and good houses; "otherwise not". | An explicit gate. "Dignity in the degrees" suggests degree-level dignity but does not name the dignities. |
| S4-15 | 125 | Opposition: sometimes brought to pass, but (in Lilly's experience) only with mutual reception by house, out of friendly houses, and the Moon separating from the quesited's significator and applying presently to the Lord of the Ascendant. Rarely seen; the querent would have done better without it (marriage quarrels; money recovered at greater legal cost). | Three joint conditions, reported as observed practice. Completion and desirability are separate results. |
| S4-16 | 125-126 | Translation, fuller form: both significators separated from conjunction, sextile or trine of each other; a planet separates from one significator, by whom it is received by house, triplicity or term, and applies to the other by conjunction or aspect before meeting the conjunction or aspect of any other planet. The translator's house rulership describes the person who brings the matter about. | Adds a principal-history condition, a directed reception condition and a no-intervening-contact condition to S4-06. No heaviness condition is stated in this passage. |
| S4-17 | 126 | Collection: the principals do not behold one another, both cast aspects to a more weighty planet than themselves, and both receive him in some essential dignity; the collector brings the matter to perfection (a third person interested in both). | Reception is by each principal of the collector. "Do not behold" is not defined as sign aversion or absence of a current aspect (A18). |
| S4-18 | 126-127 | Dwelling in houses: the quesited's significator placed in the Ascendant (e.g. Lord of the tenth for an office). Lilly: this rule of the Ancients is not true or reasonable unless the Moon, besides, transfers the light of the quesited's significator to the Lord of the Ascendant. | Rejected as an independent route. The admitted exception is a lunar translation. |
| S4-19 | 127 | Application shows the parties' inclination, separation usually privation: separated principals give little hope (notwithstanding dwelling); application means the parties seem willing and there is great probability of perfection or further treaty. | Present relation of the principals is itself testimony, distinct from completed perfection. |

## Variants and uninspected text

- **V1 (p.111):** aspectual-prohibition example, Saturn 15 Aries (S01) or 13
  Aries (S02).
- **V2 (p.111):** refranation example, sign glyph Aries (S01) or an unreadable
  glyph (S02).
- The p.112 term-reception example's blotted glyph is the same in both
  photographs and is not a P4 dependency.

No other disagreement between the witnesses was found in the passages above.
Pages 128 onward (turned houses) and the Book II worked judgements were not
re-read in this pass; they remain catalogued, not collated, for P4.
