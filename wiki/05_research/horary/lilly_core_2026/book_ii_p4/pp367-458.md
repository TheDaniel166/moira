# Lilly 1647 Book II, pp.367-410 and 432-458: P4 collation notes (reader d)

Source: Wellcome S01 photographs (`pages/s01_pNNN.jpg`, canvas = page + 34),
crops in `crops/d/`. Read every page in both ranges at full-page resolution.
Quotes keep the original spelling, with long-s written as s and planet and
aspect glyphs given as words in [brackets]. "Lilly states" means the printed
text; "my interpretation" marks my own inference.

## Q1 Translation (P05 vs P06)

1. **p.373, doctrine (lawsuit):**
   - Quote: "see if the [Moon] transferre light between the lord of the
     ascendant and seventh; if she doe not, see if any other Planet carry
     their influence or light to each other; for if it be so, it's like some
     or other interpose their paines, and reconcile the parties".
   - Bearing: no conditions are given. The use is structural (P05 style).
2. **p.376, doctrine (buying):**
   - Quote: "if the preceding Significators have no aspect to each other,
     behold if the [Moon] or some other Planet transferre not the light of
     the one to the other, a Friend shall then appeare who will drive on the
     bargaine for them both".
   - Bearing: translation is a fallback when the principals have no aspect.
     No separation, reception or lighter-planet condition is stated.
     Structural.
3. **p.383, doctrine (whether armies fight). This is the key passage.**
   - Quote: "see if any Planet transferre the light of one to the other by
     [square] or [opposition] aspect, with or without Reception; if such an
     aspect be, there will be a fight betwixt them: but if the more ponderous
     of the two receive that Planet who transferres their light, no fight will
     be, but all things will be composed lightly."
   - Lilly states:
     - Translation works by hard aspects.
     - It works "with or without Reception".
     - Reception of the translator **by the more ponderous principal**
       changes the outcome (accord instead of fight).
   - My interpretation: in this context reception is an outcome qualifier,
     not a validity condition. That is against P06 as a requirement.
4. **p.433, doctrine (ninth house, benefice). An interruption rule.**
   - Quote: "see if any Planet transferre the light of the Lord of the ninth
     to the Lord of the ascendant, the Benefice is obtained by some
     interloping person, unlesse the more ponderous Planet to whom the
     Translator doth afford vertue, commit that influence to some other
     Planet, and that Planet have no signification in the Question, for such
     an action intimates destruction of the matter, after it is hoped to be
     neer perfecting".
   - Bearing: a translation can be undone **after** the second leg if the
     heavier receiving principal passes the light on to an irrelevant planet.
     This is a new post-completion blocker, not in P05 or P06. It also names
     the receiving principal "the more ponderous Planet".
5. **p.445, doctrine (tenth house):**
   - Quote: "If any Planet transferre the vertue of the Lord of the first to
     the Lord of the tenth, then the thing will be perfected, or Office
     obtained by meanes of another".
   - Bearing: structural. No conditions. "perfected" is used of translation.
6. **p.458, doctrine (eleventh house):**
   - Quote: "any good aspect between the Lord of the ascendant and eleventh,
     or Reception or translation of light, or that the Lord of the ascendant
     be in the eleventh ... all or any of these gives hope".
   - Bearing: translation is one of several independent testimonies.
7. **C13, pp.387-388 (Lady and suitor, 16 June 1646). A worked translation.**
   - Quote: "the maine occasion, without which in this Figure it could not
     have been, the application of [Jupiter] to [sextile] of [Saturn] Lord of
     the seventh, receiving his vertue which [Saturn] did render unto him,
     and he again transferred to the [Sun] Lord of the ascendant, he, viz.
     [Jupiter], meeting with no manner of prohibition, abscission or
     frustration untill his perfect [conj] with the [Sun], which was the 29th
     of June".
   - Lilly states:
     - Jupiter is "very neer a [sextile] dexter of [Saturn], still applying,
       and not separated" (p.387). The first leg is not complete at the
       question.
     - The principals themselves are applying to a sextile: "an application
       of the two Significators to a [sextile]".
     - The direct sextile has no reception: "no Reception betwixt the
       significators, therefore that application gave little hopes".
     - The second leg is a conjunction with the Sun, and the requirement is
       no prohibition, abscission or frustration before it.
     - Timing: Lilly advises acting on 19 June, when the Saturn-Jupiter
       sextile is "perfect". The match was effected within 20 days.
   - My interpretation:
     - The figure has Sun about 5 Cancer, Jupiter 14:40 Cancer and Saturn
       14:53 Taurus, so the Sun overtakes Jupiter.
     - The translator is lighter than Saturn but heavier and slower than the
       Sun, so it is not "lighter than both" under any H01 reading.
     - The principals have not separated, so the P06 history condition is
       absent.
     - No reception of Jupiter by Saturn is cited.
     - Lilly calls the route decisive anyway.
8. **C14, p.390 (woman to marry, 11 June 1646):**
   - Quote: "I found the [Moon] separating from a [square] of [Jupiter], and
     carrying his light by a [trine] aspect to [Mercury] the Lord of the
     ascendant, and he in an angle, receiving willingly, by his Retrograde
     motion, that her vertue which she brought from [Jupiter]. I was
     confident the Match would suddenly be brought to passe by such a one as
     [Moon] was".
   - Lilly states:
     - The first leg is a separating **square**.
     - The receiving principal is retrograde, and Lilly reads that as
       favourable ("receiving willingly").
     - No essential reception is named.
     - The principals were "in a Semisextile, and in good houses".
   - My interpretation: none of the P06 conditions is used.
9. **C29, p.454:**
   - Quote: "the [Moon] transferres the influence of [Jupiter] to [Saturn],
     by a forcible and strong aspect, viz. a [trine]".
   - Bearing: a descriptive translation by trine. Structural.
10. **C26, p.438, listed as a negative:**
    - Quote: "There is no weighty Planet that translates or collects the
      light of [Jupiter] (who signifies the preferment) unto [Mars]."
    - Bearing: "weighty" qualifies translation and collection together. It
      may mean "of weight" (efficacious). Read literally, it calls the
      translator heavy. Treat it as ambiguous.

**My conclusion for Q1:**

- In this range Lilly never requires the principals to have separated first.
  C13 has them applying.
- He never requires reception for validity: "with or without Reception",
  p.383.
- He never states a lighter-translator condition, and C13's translator is
  not the lightest.
- He does require an uninterrupted second leg (C13).
- Overall this is P05-structural plus a no-interruption condition. Reception
  by the heavier principal is an outcome qualifier (p.383), and the heavier
  principal passing the light on can undo the route (p.433).

## Q2 Prohibition (B01/B02)

1. **p.388, C13:**
   - Quote: "[Mars] was neerer [Venus] then [Saturn], and so interposed his
     influence, or kept off [Saturn]".
   - Bearing: the interposer lies between the applying Saturn and the target
     Venus. It is described as contacting the target first. Nothing about
     touching both.
2. **p.433, doctrine:**
   - Quote: "If the Lord of the ascendant hasten to the [conj] of the Lord of
     the ninth, and meet with no obstructive aspect before, it's not then to
     be doubted".
   - Bearing: an **aspect** met by the **applying** planet before the
     conjunction obstructs.
3. **p.445, doctrine:**
   - Quote: "doth really come to [conj] of the Lord of the tenth, without the
     abscission of any other Planet before [conj]".
   - Bearing: "abscission" by another planet before the conjunction. The
     subject is the applying Lord of the ascendant.
4. **p.374, doctrine:** "so that no other Planet hinder their aspect".
   Generic.
5. **p.402, C20:**
   - Quote: "the application of the [Moon] was so directly to the [sextile]
     of the Lord of the ascendant, without any frustration or prohibition".
   - Bearing: prohibition and frustration are checked on a **sextile**
     application.
6. **Inverse cases (benefic interjection as rescue):**
   - p.444, C28: "no Fortune interjecting his benevolent aspect betwixt
     [Mercury] and the two Infortunes".
   - p.433: "If that Infortune commit his vertue to any Fortune, who is
     strong ... the thing will be perfected".

**My conclusion for Q2:**

- Bodily and aspectual interposition are both used.
- The texts frame the interposition as met by the applier (p.433) or as
  getting to the target first (p.388).
- No passage requires contact with both principals.
- Swiftness of the interposer is never mentioned.
- This leans to `interposition_to_either`.

## Q3 Refranation and frustration (B03/B04)

1. **p.373, doctrine:**
   - Quote: "see if the lord of the ascendant hastens to the [conj] of the
     lord of the seventh, or the lord of the seventh to him, and that the
     lord of the tenth house doth frustrate their [conj], it's then an
     argument they shall not agree before they have been at law".
   - Lilly states:
     - Frustration is of a **conjunction**.
     - The frustrator is the third significator (the judge).
     - Either principal may be the applier.
     - The effect is delay and a detour (law first), not outright denial.
   - Which principal the frustrator touches is not stated.
2. **p.402, C20:** "without any frustration or prohibition", said of a
   **sextile**.
   - My interpretation: frustration is checked on aspects in case practice,
     but this is a loose usage, not a definition.
3. **p.409, doctrine:** "for when their perfect [conj] is, whether by body or
   aspect".
   - Bearing: "conjunction" is used generically for perfection by aspect.
   - My interpretation: this weakens a strictly bodily reading of
     "frustrate their [conj]" on p.373.
4. **Refranation:** the word does not occur in either range.
5. **Stations and ingress (related usage):**
   - **p.443, C28:** "[Mercury] lately Retrograde, and in [square] of [Mars],
     now moving slowly in his direct motion, and applying againe to a second
     [square]". A station and re-application is read as a renewed attempt,
     not as refranation.
   - **Pp.433-434 (collector):** the collector must not "become Retrograde
     before he goe out of the Signe he is in".
   - **P.446:** the collector must not be "Retrograde, Combust or Cadent, or
     goe out of that Signe wherein he is before the [conj] of the Lord of the
     first and tenth with him".
   - **P.447 (receiver of the disposition):** the officer stays "untill that
     Receiver become Retrograde or approach to Combustion, or goe out of the
     Signe wherein he is".
   - Bearing: retrogradation, combustion or a sign change of the
     **receiving or collecting** planet ends the route or effect.
   - This is relevant to K05 (ingress not a blocker): for collection, Lilly
     states ingress of the collector before both principals join it as a
     defeating condition.

## Q4 Heaviness (H01)

1. **The more ponderous planet is the receiver of the disposition.**
   - p.447: "see if the more ponderous Planet of the two, that is, the
     receiver of the Disposition be in any angle".
   - p.449: "the more ponderous of them who receives the disposition of the
     more light Planet".
   - p.433: "the more ponderous Planet to whom the Translator doth afford
     vertue".
   - Bearing: Lilly identifies the heavier planet functionally, as the one
     applied to. He gives no ordering rule.
2. **"More weighty" and "superiour" as synonyms.**
   - p.372: "unity shall proceed from that party whose Significator is lesse
     ponderous, and commits his disposition to the other ... If the lighter
     Planet be joyned to the more weighty, and receive him not, but the
     superiour Planet receive him, it argues, he that receives would accord".
   - Bearing: "more weighty" and "superiour Planet" are used as synonyms in
     one sentence.
   - My interpretation: this points to an order-based or superior-planet
     sense of weight, and possibly "superior" in the sense of the receiving
     party.
3. **Lightness comparisons with no method stated:**
   - p.373: "if the lord of the ascendant be more light, and the lord of the
     seventh more ponderous, the first motion of peace shall come from the
     querent".
   - p.374: "If the lord of the tenth be more light then the lord of the
     ascendant, and joyned unto him".
   - p.376: "if the Lord of the ascendant be a more light Planet then the
     Lord of the seventh, the querent will occasion the sale".
   - p.432: "if the Lord of the ninth be the lighter Planet, and apply to
     the Lord of the first".
   - p.444: "The Lord of the tenth in the first, so he be a lighter Planet
     then the Lord of the first, though no aspect be betwixt them, yet shall
     he attain". Heaviness is used here without any aspect.
   - p.449: "If the Lord of the tenth be more light then the Lord of the
     fourth ... more light then the Lord of the first".
4. **C13, p.387:** "she is signified by the lighter Planet". She is the Sun,
   compared with Saturn. All readings agree.
5. **Weight linked to slowness:**
   - p.439: Jupiter is "a slow and ponderous Planet".
   - p.447: the receiver "slow in motion".
6. **p.456, C30:** "[Saturn] being a ponderous Planet, made me more confident
   her Majesty would move forwards".
   - Context: Saturn is "removing out of one Signe into another" (into
     Aries).
   - Bearing: "ponderous" is a quality testifying to steady progress, not a
     comparison.
   - **Not found:** any "Saturn though a ponderous planet yet being
     retrograde" passage.
7. **p.380:** "a light Planet and unfortunate", used of the Lord of the
   ascendant. Here "light" seems to mean weak.

## Q5 Collection (P07)

1. **p.375, doctrine:** "if both Significators give their vertue to one
   Planet, there will be one who will intercede betwixt them". No
   conditions.
2. **Pp.433-434, doctrine:**
   - Quote: "if the Lord of the first and ninth both of them commit their
     disposition to any Planet, by whatsoever aspect, who is not impedited,
     or becomes Retrograde before he goe out of the Signe he is in, it argues
     performance".
   - Bearing: any aspect; the collector unimpedited and not turning
     retrograde before leaving its sign. No non-beholding, heaviness or
     reception condition.
3. **p.446, doctrine:**
   - Quote: "If the Lord of the ascendant and tenth commit their disposition
     to any Planet by any aspect, with or with no Reception, whether the
     Receiver be a Fortune or Infortune (so that he be not Retrograde,
     Combust or Cadent, or goe out of that Signe wherein he is before the
     [conj] of the Lord of the first and tenth with him) and if the [Moon] be
     joyned to the Lord of the first or tenth, the querent shall atcheive the
     preferment".
   - Bearing:
     - Reception of the collector is **explicitly not required**.
     - The collector's conditions are stated.
     - There is a lunar side-condition.
4. **p.448:** "If the Lord of the first or the [Moon] be joyned to the Lord of
   the tenth or either of them, and he more weighty then either of them, and
   be in a good place of heaven". The receiver is heavier.
5. **p.438, C26:** "no weighty Planet that translates or collects". Supports
   a heavier collector.
6. **"Behold" and "not behold":**
   - p.379: "if the Lord of the fourth be in such houses as behold not the
     fourth".
   - p.448: "though he behold not the tenth".
   - p.449: "do not aspect the tenth house".
   - Bearing: these are used of a planet relative to a **house or cusp**. The
     cases also aspect cusps by degree: p.393 trine to the 6th cusp; p.453
     trines to the 10th cusp; p.455 "wants six degrees of being partill" to
     the 11th cusp.
   - No passage in this range says "do not behold" of two principals as a
     collection condition.

## Q6 Good, friendly and proper houses (Q01)

1. **p.448:** "a good place of heaven, viz. either in the tenth, eleventh, or
   fifth free from all manner of impediments". An explicit list, in a
   tenth-house context.
2. **p.390, C14:** "both Significators ... in good houses". No list.
3. **p.379, siege:** "or in any house except the twelft, eight and sixt".
   - My interpretation: a negative list.
4. **Angle, succedent and cadent:**
   - p.433: "in an angle; hastens the matter, in a succedant, it retards the
     thing; in a cadent, quite destroyes the matter; or suddenly, beyond
     expectation, when all men despair, by some secret trick, perfects it;
     but this is very rare".
   - p.446: "In every Question you must observe, that what Planet soever is
     Significator of any thing, if he be in an angle, he hastens to effect
     the matter; in a succedant, the matter goes on slowly; in a cadent
     house, the matter goes backward and backward, yet at last is
     performed".
   - Bearing: the two pages give different cadent outcomes. P.446 is
     general, for any significator, not only a conjunction (P01).
5. **p.368, marginal note:** planets in houses 10, 11, 12, 1, 2, 3 favour
   the querent's side and 9 down to 4 the quesited's (war context). Not a
   "good houses" definition.

## Q7 Reception overriding (P08)

1. **p.372:**
   - Quote: "If they, viz. the lord of the seventh and ascendant are in a
     [square] aspect, or in [opposition], with mutuall Reception, or in a
     [sextile] or [trine] without Reception, they will be reconciled, but
     first they will have one little combate at law".
   - Bearing: mutual reception upgrades a hard aspect to the level of an
     unreceived soft one.
   - One-way reception: "they shall agree without Suit of law, but not
     without intermission of a third party".
2. **p.385:** "the [square] aspect shewes envy and malice, yet posible to be
   reconciled, [opposition] aspects without Reception, never".
3. **p.433:**
   - Quote: "If the aspect be by [square], it comes not so lightly; yet if
     Reception be, it comes at length".
   - Quote: "the [conj] of the Infortunes without Reception, performes
     nothing; with Reception, they performe with difficulty".
4. **Reception with no aspect stated:**
   - p.445: "If the Lord of the tenth receive the Lord of the first or the
     [Moon] by any reception, or in any house, the matter will be effected
     with much content and profit".
   - p.446: "if the Planet so posited do receive the [Moon] or Lord of the
     first, the matter will be perfected, but without Reception, not".
   - p.449: "if the Lord of the first be received of a Planet not impedited,
     he returnes: if not received, no returne".
5. **p.458:**
   - Reception is listed alone among the testimonies.
   - Quote: "if the aspect be with [square] or [opposition], he shall get it
     with much labor and tediousnesse; yea, although there be Reception of
     Significators".
6. **Counter-instance: C27, p.441.** "there's Reception betwixt the [Moon] and
   him, but no aspect". The judgement is negative.
7. **C13, p.387:** the application without reception "gave little hopes".
   Reception is weighed, not treated as an override.
8. **C20, p.402:** "because [Mars] and the [Moon] were in Reception ... I
   judged his Excellency should obtaine". Reception plus an applying
   sextile.
9. **p.408 (degree of reception required):** "joyned to an Infortune, who
   receives him not either by House or Exaltation, or by two of his lesser
   Dignities". Lesser dignities count only in pairs here.

**My conclusion for Q7:**

- Reception upgrades hard aspects.
- Some tenth- and eleventh-house rules accept reception, or reception plus
  house placement, without an aspect. C27 shows that is not automatic.
- No passage says reception overrides prohibition, frustration or
  refranation.

## Q8 Conjunction house class, malevolent aspect (P01/B05)

1. **House class:** see Q6 (pp.433, 446). Lilly states the rule for any
   significator, not specifically for a conjunction.
2. **"Malevolent aspect":**
   - p.409: "or if they were in [square] or [opposition] aspect, then when
     the malevolent Interfector comes to the degree ...". Also: "meets with
     the malevolent aspect of the Lord of the sixt".
   - p.433: "If any malevolent Planet aspect the Lord of the ascendant or
     the [Moon] with [square] or [opposition], without Reception".
   - p.446: "See if an ill Planet behold the Lord of the ascendant or the
     [Moon], with [square] or [opposition], without Reception, for unlesse
     he then commit his disposition to another, he hinders".
   - My interpretation:
     - Lilly's hindering contacts pair a malefic planet **with** a hard
       aspect, without reception. B05 stays unresolved.
     - P.446 adds an escape: the malefic "commit his disposition to
       another".

## Q9 Other points

1. **Routes as independent testimonies with no ranking.**
   - p.458: aspect, or reception, or translation, or house placement.
   - p.444: a fallback sequence. Conjunction of the Lord of the first and
     the Moon with the Lord of the tenth. Else the Lord of the first or the
     Moon in the 10th, "if that Planet be not impedited". Else the Lord of
     the tenth in the 1st, if lighter, "though no aspect be betwixt them".
2. **C13:** the translation is named "the maine occasion, without which ...
   it could not have been", even though the direct sextile was also applying.
   - My interpretation: a translation can be judged decisive alongside a
     pending direct aspect. That supports K04 (report each route; no
     pre-emption by a direct application).
3. **Disposition chain, p.445:** Lord of the first, then a malefic, then
   another malefic, then a Fortune, then the Lord of the tenth: "imports
   acquisition of the Dignity, but with infinite perplexities".
4. **Separation as present relation:**
   - p.384: separated means past contest; applying means approaching.
   - p.447: "But if they are seperated from each other, then he returnes
     not".
   - p.454: "[Venus] is in [square] of [Mars], but separated", read as a past
     fight.
   - p.376: the planets the Moon separates from and applies to "are equally
     significant, as the ascendant and seventh".
5. **C20 hypothetical, p.403:** "in regard the [Moon] applies to a [sextile]
   of [Mars], the querent therefore would have had the victory", but
   overruled by the Sun (judge) in the 7th. A direct application is
   outweighed by other testimony.

## Non-findings (read, nothing on routes)

- **Pp.367-371:** lawsuit strength, partnership, neighbours, removal.
  Dignity and strength comparisons only.
- **Pp.377-382, 384:** partnership, siege, commanders, enemies.
  - Reception conditions only: p.377 reception "by House or Exaltation, or
    Triplicity or Terme"; p.379 one-way reception "though she be not received
    againe".
- **Pp.391-399:** C15 fugitive, C16 dog, C17 money, C18 fish. Significator
  selection and direct applications only (p.391 "hastening to a [trine] out
  of angles"; p.396 timing by degrees).
- **Pp.399-400, C19 (Waller and Hopton):** lunar separation and application;
  Saturn "in no aspect of any Planet".
- **Pp.404-407:** sickness and death. Aspect timing only. P.405: "if he be
  but going unto, and is not separated from the aspect, he hath not been, but
  he will be suddenly ill".
- **Pp.434-437, 439-442:** dreams, voyages, C27 Presbytery (conjectural;
  Moon void then squares).
- **Pp.450-451:** profession.
