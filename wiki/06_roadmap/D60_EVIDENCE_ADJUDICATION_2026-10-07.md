## Published arudha-point pair and its precision limit

Rao, [*Padamsas and Transits: A Research Finding*](https://vedicastrologer.org/articles/padamsa_transit.pdf),
1 February 2010, version 2; 15 pages; SHA-256
`4b4c9dc1f3cd798b7fad0f96e3f0df3026e94344d269ae2093b3f11ab42a147d`.
PDF pp.2-3 define mapping an already calculated rasi-level pada as a point,
as with a planet, into a divisional chart. Example 30, **PDF p.12**, prints
the twelfth-house pada at **16 Cancer 17 in D1** and **16 Pisces 37 in D60**.
The article states Jagannatha ayanamsa and true nodes. Its minute-rounding
rule is not stated. The governing calculation/example pages were visually checked.

These are published coordinates of the **same mathematical point**. They
were not recovered by inverting an unpaired D60 chart. Feeding the printed
D1 center into Moira gives **17 Pisces 00**, a difference of **23 D60
arcminutes**. It therefore fails an exact printed-number comparison.

If both coordinates were rounded to the nearest arcminute, the D1 interval
is `[16 Cancer 16m30s, 16 Cancer 17m30s)`. The composed profile maps that
interval to `[16 Pisces 30m, 17 Pisces 30m)`. The published D60 interval,
`[16 Pisces 36m30s, 16 Pisces 37m30s)`, lies inside it. Under truncation to
minutes, the D1 interval instead maps to `[17 Pisces 00, 18 Pisces 00)`;
compatibility fails. This conditional result is recorded explicitly. There
is no fitted tolerance, fabricated precise D1 input or rounding in runtime.

One even-sign arudha point cannot establish odd/even parity coverage, planetary
ephemeris accuracy or an independently reproduced chart. Source-owned birth
data are context; we do not substitute Lahiri for its declared frame. The
source's pada construction, house doctrine and predictive assertions are not
admitted by a test of the point mapping.
