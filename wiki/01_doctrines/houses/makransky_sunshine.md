# Makransky Sunshine

[Bob Makransky’s published exposition](https://www.astro.nu/2017/03/03/astrology-house-systems/) governs this construction. The Sun’s declination circle and the local geometric horizon determine the solar day and night arcs. Each arc supplies six equal intervals in hour angle.

With latitude φ and solar declination δ, the altitude equation at the horizon gives cos(H) = −tan(φ)tan(δ). The two distinct roots are sunrise at −H and sunset at +H, with west-positive hour angle. Each subdivision point determines a plane through the local north–south horizon axis. The ecliptic intersects the plane in antipodal directions. The projected boundary belongs to the same horizon hemisphere as the solar subdivision point. At sunrise and sunset, select the eastern and western horizon intersections respectively.

Numbering starts at sunrise and proceeds backward in hour angle through the night, reaching sunset at house 7, then traverses the day back toward sunrise. The upper and lower meridian crossings define houses 10 and 4. The cycle must be strictly ordered with its four angular cusps on the chart angles.

Moira requires two distinct geometric horizon crossings. Polar day, polar night, tangent contact, and degenerate or inconsistent projection raise an explicit error. This is Moira’s domain policy for a twelve-house result. The frame uses geocentric apparent solar declination of date and a geometric horizon.

Code `MS` identifies this construction. Legacy code `N` identifies Sun-Anchored Equal, with the Sun on cusp 12. Low-level ARMC callers provide solar declination for `MS`. Epoch-backed calculation obtains it from the ephemeris when no override is supplied.

The printed sixth cusp is inconsistent with the stated equal division. At the printed MC of 25°52′ Cancer, latitude 48°15′ N and obliquity 23.45°, the fifth cusp implies a solar declination of +11.76° and the sixth +5.38°. To recover those values independently of the cusp solver, express a printed cusp direction in local axes and form its zenith component divided by its east component. A house plane through north fixes that ratio. If `L` is the solar half-night arc and `f` is the fraction from lower transit toward sunset, the ratio for a solar point is `cos(latitude) × (cos(fL) − cos(L)) / sin(fL)`. Solving for `L` with `f = 1/3` for cusp 5 and `f = 2/3` for cusp 6 gives 76.51° and 83.94° respectively. Then `tan(declination) = cos(L) / tan(latitude)`. Checking ±0.5′ around the printed MC and cusp values at obliquities 23.44° and 23.46° leaves the inferred declinations separated by more than 6°. Both cusps must share one solar half-night arc.

Reconstructing the example with DE441 at 17:03:27 UT on 1889-04-20, latitude 48°15′ N and longitude 13°02′ E gives a solar declination of +11.75288°. This interprets the article’s rectified 18:03:27 on its reported GDT clock, GMT+1. The resulting MC is 25°52′30″ Cancer and eleven cusps agree within 1.01′. Cusp 6 comes out at 0°39′34″ Aries, 79.56′ beyond the printed 29°20′ Pisces. Treating the rectified clock value as local mean time instead gives 17:11:19 UT and moves the MC to about 27°45′ Cancer. The GDT interpretation accounts for the rest of the table. The evidence isolates the published sixth row as an outlier. Whether it arose in calculation or transcription remains unknown.

The sixth-row residual has the signature of a sign reversal at the Pisces–Aries boundary. The calculated signed longitude is +0.659354°. Negating it and wrapping to [0°, 360°) gives 359.340646°, or 29°20′26″ Pisces. That rounds to the printed 29°20′ Pisces and misses the printed value by only 26″. The independent arc check selects the Aries side. This identifies the numerical transformation that reproduces the discrepancy. The published material does not establish where the sign was reversed.

Makransky’s [June/July 2021 Astrological Journal table](https://www.astro.com/astrology/aa_article210701_e.htm?nho2=302&nhor=301) gives the same chart’s sixth cusp as **0°38′ Aries**, confirming the Aries side in a later author publication. It also revises cusp 5 to 4°44′ Pisces and cusp 12 to 26°30′ Virgo. Against this version, all twelve DE441 reconstruction residuals are at most 1.57′. Cusp 6 differs by about 1′34″. The 2017 table remains evidence of the publication discrepancy.

The [book appendix](https://studylibit.com/doc/7583522/bob-markansky---primary-directions#:~:text=SOLAR%20HOUSE%20CUSP%20ALGORITHM), pp. 147–148, publishes a separate numerical case under “Solar House Cusp Algorithm.” For ARMC 12.37°, latitude 51.50°, and declination −18.38°, all eight intermediate cusps match within 0.91′ using an assumed obliquity of 23.45°. The following worked direction on p. 149 supplies a different witness for the same chart. From the solar-circle point at RA 20.86°, Moira’s geometric construction gives pole height 17.46025° and equator-intersection RA 26.85896°, matching the printed 17.46° and 26.86° at their stated precision.

The implementation’s loop follows the two directed arcs and owns each shared endpoint once. It uses Moira’s existing horizon vectors and plane intersection primitives. Each antipodal choice follows the horizon hemisphere of the sampled direction, with east and west fixing the two horizon endpoints. The order and angular checks reject an invalid result at assembly. A spatial witness can reconstruct the same plane from its returned ecliptic cusp and the north–south axis, since the accepted intersection is nondegenerate. Verification derives the test subdivision angles with an independent bisection of the altitude equation and checks plane incidence directly. Makransky’s exposition supplies the construction and the printed comparison values.

## Printed recipe and geometric domain

The comparison uses Makransky’s [original appendix scan, pp. 147–148](https://web.archive.org/web/20160419014341id_/http://www.dearbrutus.com/primarydirections_3.pdf#page=44). The scalar transcription follows the printed branches, normalizes angles modulo 360°, and interprets the instruction for `Z > 90°` as a reflection about the stated zodiacal quarter point. The printed test frame agrees with the vector calculation to floating-point precision. A research grid of 14,904 frames sampled ARMC every 5°, 23 latitudes from −89° to +89°, and nine declinations from −23.44° to +23.44° at 23.45° obliquity. All 46,080 comparable northern and equatorial intermediate cusps agreed within 3.66 × 10⁻¹⁰°.

**High latitude.** Step 4 gives formulas for meridian distances below or equal to 90°. The outer subdivision of a semi-arc is two thirds of that arc, so the longer semi-arc reaches the edge of the printed recipe at 135°. Equivalently, the gap begins when `abs(tan(latitude) × tan(declination)) > sqrt(1/2)`. At declination magnitude 23.44°, this is beyond about 58.49° latitude, before solar tangency at 66.56°. At 65° N with ARMC 37° and declination +23.44°, cusps 8 and 12 fall into that gap. Their house circles still intersect the ecliptic, giving 348.572610° and 126.692073°. Declination −23.44° puts cusps 2 and 6 in the gap instead. At 80° N with ARMC 37° and declination +5°, all eight intermediate cusps agree. At ARMC 270° in that frame, the chart MC is below the horizon. Moira refuses the full cycle because the construction assigns house 10 to the day arc and requires the MC as its cusp. Step 9 also leaves exact `W = 180°` without a branch. The plane construction handles that case when the intersection is otherwise valid.

**Southern latitudes.** The appendix prescribes substituting RAIC for RAMC and shifting the resulting cusps by 180°. Its latitude and declination signs are implicit. Keeping latitude signed agreed on every available printed cusp in 5,300 of 5,376 southern frames accepted by Moira. The other 76 hit the exact `W = 90°` or `W = 270°` branch at zero declination. For ARMC 30°, latitude −45°, and declination 0°, the signed pole changes the direction of the printed correction, yielding cusp 6 at 289.015673°. Direct projection yields 250.984327° and varies continuously on either side of that ARMC. Taking latitude positive while retaining declination introduces larger disagreements even away from those exact branches. As a separate geometric check, reversing latitude and solar declination together, shifting ARMC by 180°, and rotating the output cusps back is a consistent antipodal transformation. It brought all 40,968 comparable southern cusps into agreement within 3.66 × 10⁻¹⁰°. That sign transformation is a derived diagnostic.

The regression cases solve the horizon crossing from the altitude equation and verify that each selected cusp and its assigned solar point lie in the same north–south house plane. They also check the horizon hemisphere of each cusp, the four angular anchors, continuity through the southern cardinal case, and the refusal to produce a figure for polar day, polar night, tangency or incompatible chart angles. These geometric checks cover the cases where the printed scalar recipe has a gap or gives a different value.
