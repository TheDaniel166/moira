# Porphyry (5° Prespan)

The *Introduction to the Tetrabiblos* attributed to Porphyry divides the ecliptic quadrants into thirds and places five zodiacal degrees before the angular reference inside each angular house. Chapter 43 rejects a proportional one-sixth prespan. Chapter 52 explains how to distribute the widths around the circle and gives a worked set of unequal quadrant spans. Moira uses the descriptive label **Porphyry (5° Prespan)** and code `P5`. [Greek text, chapter 43](https://www.wilbourhall.org/astrology/CCAGTome05Part4.pdf#page=112) · [Greek text, chapter 52](https://www.wilbourhall.org/astrology/CCAGTome05Part4.pdf#page=117) · [Holden translation, chapter 52](https://biblioteca.saturnalia.com.br/Introduction-to-the-Tetrabiblos.pdf#page=60)

## Geometry and domain

Use Moira's astronomical Ascendant, IC, Descendant and MC in forward ecliptic order. Divide each successive arc into three widths, and accumulate those widths from ASC − 5°. Equivalently, translate the twelve ordinary Porphyry openings by −5°. The angles remain astronomical references and the shifted openings govern placement. Apply any zodiac offset to both sets.

Moira requires a uniquely defined rising intersection and distinct, ordered angles. A quadrant must exceed 15° so each third exceeds 5° and the angular house extends past its angle. A quadrant at or below 15°, a coincident horizon and ecliptic, or a site at an exact geographic pole raises a domain error. These conditions are Moira's explicit admission policy for the construction.

The chapter 52 example gives successive forward widths of 84°, 96°, 84° and 96° starting at the Ascendant. With ASC 3° and MC 267° chosen to exercise the wrap, the openings are 358°, 26°, 54°, 82°, 114°, 146°, 178°, 206°, 234°, 262°, 294° and 326°. The source supplies the spans. The absolute anchor longitudes are test inputs.
