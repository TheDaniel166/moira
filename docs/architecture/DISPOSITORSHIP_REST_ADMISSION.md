# Dispositorship REST admission

`admit_after_minor_engine_work` for the read-only comparison, subsystem, and
condition network over caller-supplied ecliptic longitudes. The public
`moira.dignities` entry points own traversal, policy validation, terminal
cycles, local condition states, and directed relations. The
[backend standard](../../wiki/02_standards/DISPOSITORSHIP_BACKEND_STANDARD.md)
governs Classic 7 subjects, traditional domicile, self-domiciled finals,
terminal cycles, and unsupported-subject handling. These are synchronous,
kernel-free projections of one bounded selection of planets.

Two reproducible engine failures precede transport admission:

- A subsystem with caller-ordered Saturn and Sun rejects the chart condition
  profile because that profile requires dignity order. Local conditions must
  follow the chain order. Chart conditions and network nodes must follow
  dignity order. Cross-layer alignment should compare subject evidence at the
  appropriate ordering boundary.
- A self-domiciled Sun at 140 degrees and an out-of-scope Pluto at 221 degrees
  trigger the mixed-terminals invariant under the default policy. The chart
  flag must use unresolved in-scope conditions, as the result vessel requires.

Admission witnesses include default parity with Natal Discovery and controlled
fixtures for a final, a reciprocal cycle, a missing ruler, and an
out-of-scope subject. Explicit reject and segregate policies, both result
orders, and completed empty selections need distinct transport coverage.

The transport binds the public comparison, subsystem, and network reducers
under `/v1/dignities/dispositorship`. An ordered array of up to ten distinct
canonical planet names and longitudes makes caller ordering explicit.
The comparison accepts one to eight named policies and applies them to
the same array. The engine validates selector combinations and determines
result order. Serializers retain each policy, the supplied positions,
and the engine's distinction between unresolved and out-of-scope subjects.
